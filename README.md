Disaster Law and Policy
Course wiki for Disaster Law and Policy: International and Comparative Perspectives, a 30 hour elective taught in English at FGV Direito Rio. Instead of a linear reading list, the course materials are published as a small graph: each work, author, concept, legal instrument and case is a page, and the links between pages are typed relations, so a case you reach from one session also shows you the other sessions that cite it.

Read it at https://leocarrilho7-lab.github.io/Disaster-Law-and-Policy/.

What is in here
This repository holds the generated site, rebuilt as a whole from a single source. The pages fall into six kinds:

Sessions	the syllabus unit, with its computed bibliography
Reading notes	working analyses of the assigned texts, with page level citations
Authors	who wrote what, and where each work enters the syllabus
Concepts	the analytical vocabulary of the course, linked to the texts that carry it
Legal instruments	Sendai, the Paris Agreement, ISO 31000 and other global frameworks
Cases	leading decisions cited in the readings
graph.html draws the whole network and lets you switch node types off, which is the fastest way to see how texts and concepts relate once authors and sessions are out of the way. busca.html searches everything in the browser.

Nothing here calls out to the network: no fonts, no analytics, no content delivery network, no tracking of any kind. Download the repository, open index.html, and the wiki works offline.

The three reliability marks
A reading note mixes things checked in the source with things inferred by whoever wrote the note, and it says which is which. Hover a mark to see what was consulted, where and when.

✓ checked in the source
? inference of the note, not a statement of the text
✗ not verified yet
The marks are derived from a register of verifications, never typed by hand. A mark whose register is missing falls back to "not verified" on its own, so a decorative mark cannot pass for a checked fact.

The wiki grows one session at a time
A session goes online after it has been taught, never before. This is not a display rule. The pages of a session that has not happened yet are never generated, so they are not in this repository at all, hidden or otherwise, and reading the page source will not find them. Sessions 4, 6 and 9 are in that state today: the works they use appear here as bibliographic entries, with title and author and no analysis.

The Markdown sources, the course reader and the working notes live in a separate private repository. What you see here is the published output.

Language
Reading notes are written in English, with a Portuguese version behind the language toggle at the top of each page. The navigation labels are still in Portuguese, and that is a pending job, not a design choice.

Corrections
Open an issue. A reading note is a reading, and a reading can be wrong. The most useful correction is the one that shows a mark of a checked fact standing on a source that does not say it.
