S03: Risk, Uncertainty, Vulnerability and Resilience

How this works

  1. You play in a GROUP of up to three. Pick one nickname and one symbol; once a group takes a symbol, it is gone.
  2. The system deals your case. You do not choose it, and two groups may end up with the same one.
  3. The case gives real facts up to the moment a decision had to be taken. You are never told what happened next.
  4. Your job is to name the REGIME OF UNCERTAINTY the decision sits in, and then the rule that regime commands.
  5. Then new information arrives. You judge whether it makes your decision more or less defensible, and decide whether to move.
  6. Every step has a clock, and the activity has one too. When the activity clock runs out the game closes and shows your board, finished or not.
  7. There is no speed score and no individual ranking. What counts is whether the group moves when the evidence says move.

Dealing...

The drainage that was changed

An iron ore tailings dam has been raised eight times by the upstream method. A design change moved the drainage system, and piezometers now read saturation above the level the original project assumed. The operator applies to renew the operating licence. The agency has three technical staff for four hundred dams in the state.

Name the one fact that, if false, would change your layer.

The study that stayed internal

A coastal nuclear plant is licensed against a design-basis tsunami of 5.7 metres, derived from the instrumental record. An internal study, using paleotsunami deposits rather than instruments, suggests waves of up to 15.7 metres are possible. The study is not peer reviewed. Raising the seawall would take four years and halt one reactor.

Does a non-peer-reviewed study change the regime, or only the parameter?

Six months of small tremors

A medieval town has felt minor earthquakes for six months. Seismologists agree on the number: the swarm raises the probability of a destructive event in the next week from about 1 in 200,000 to about 1 in 10,000. Nobody disputes the estimate. A public official must decide whether to tell the town, and in what words. Evacuation costs money and credibility; silence has, elsewhere, ended in prosecutions.

If the number is agreed, name exactly what is still uncertain.

The barrier and the century

A tidal barrier protects a capital city and was designed for a sea level rise that current projections have already overtaken at the upper bound. Replacement takes twenty-five years and one budget cycle cannot fund it. Projections for 2100 differ by a factor of three depending on ice-sheet assumptions that no observation can yet settle.

Draw the decision you would take in year 1 and the one you would keep open for year 15.

The test that read wrong

On a deepwater well, a negative pressure test gives an anomalous reading. One crew reads it as a failed barrier; another reads it as an artefact of the line. Displacement is scheduled to begin in two hours, and the rig is forty-three days behind schedule at high daily cost.

What would you require before proceeding, and what does requiring it cost?

Week three

A respiratory pathogen with no prior human circulation has been reported in one country. The case fatality rate is estimated from hospitalised patients only. Whether it transmits before symptoms appear is unknown and will remain unknown for at least a month. Border and school measures must be decided this week or not at all.

Which of your unknowns will resolve on its own, and which only resolve if you act?

Round 0: what makes this hard?

Read your Case card. In two minutes and in silence, write the things that make this decision hard. Three words each, as many as you have. Then, as a group, send the three your group keeps.

Round 1: name the regime

Read your Case card. Place it on ONE layer of the mat and write the decision that layer commands. Then write the strongest argument AGAINST your own choice. Commit before the signal.

Round 2: the signal arrives

Turn over the Signal card. Independently, and before your group speaks: does this information make your Round 1 decision more or less defensible?
