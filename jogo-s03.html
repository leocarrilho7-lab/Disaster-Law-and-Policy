S03: Risk, Uncertainty, Vulnerability and Resilience

How this works

  1. You play in a GROUP of up to three. Pick one nickname and one symbol; once a group takes a symbol, it is gone.
  2. The system deals your case. You do not choose it, and two groups may end up with the same one.
  3. The case gives real facts up to the moment a decision had to be taken. You are never told what happened next.
  4. Your job is to name the REGIME OF UNCERTAINTY the decision sits in, and then the rule that regime commands.
  5. Then new information arrives. You judge whether it makes your decision more or less defensible, and decide whether to move.
  6. Every step has a clock, and the activity has one too. When the activity clock runs out the game closes and shows your board, finished or not.
  7. There is no speed score and no individual ranking. What counts is whether the group moves when the evidence says move.

This session is also a study. If your group agrees, the answers you give here may be used, without your names, in research on how people judge uncertainty.

Saying no changes nothing: you play the whole game either way, and it does not affect your grade. You can tell the instructor at any time and we drop your group's answers.

Dealing...

The drainage that was changed

An iron ore tailings dam has been raised eight times by the upstream method, each lift founded on the tailings the lift before it laid down. Two changes were made to the structure as built. A construction defect in the base drain made the original drainage concept unbuildable, and the replacement blanket drain was rebuilt at a higher elevation than the one it replaced. Separately, a structural problem in a concrete conduit running under the dam forced the alignment of the embankment to be set back, and the setback left part of the structure resting on soft slimes that the design had never intended to load. Piezometers now read saturation above the level the original project assumed, and at the last review the replacement drain was at the limit of what it can carry. The operator applies to renew the operating licence. The agency has three technical staff for four hundred dams in the state. The two engineering teams accept the same piezometer readings and the same rainfall series. What they do not share is the structure those readings belong to: one team treats the embankment as drained, the other as saturated through the setback zone, and each reading of the structure implies a different failure mode. Both can say what they would do, and neither disputes what the other values.

The defence and the tariff

A coastal municipality must choose between raising a flood defence and continuing to pay compensation for the flooding it allows. The events at issue are shallow tidal floods rather than extremes: they have arrived in most years for four decades, and the tide gauge has recorded every one of them. The defence has a costed price and a known effect on flooded area. Two consultancies nevertheless report different annual rates, each can say what record would settle the difference, and the council is advised to wait for better science before committing capital.

The reserve that study cannot shrink

A state civil defence agency must fix by regulation the standing reserve it holds through the rainy season: shelter capacity, crews on call, and stock that cannot be assembled once a season has begun. The seasonal average number of qualifying events is contested. One group of hydrologists puts it near eleven, another near fourteen, and each side can say what record would move the figure. A measurement campaign has been proposed to settle it, and the agency is asked to defer the regulation until that campaign reports. The reserve is expensive to hold and every year it goes unused is a year someone calls it waste.

Six months of small tremors

A medieval town has felt minor earthquakes for six months. The seismologists agree on what the science says and on where it stops. The swarm has raised the probability of a destructive shock by a large factor, plausibly as much as a hundredfold above the long-term average for this fault, and the absolute probability over the coming week is still small, as it remains even in the most seismically active places. They agree too that on present knowledge a warning would be far more likely to turn out a false alarm than silence would turn out a failure to warn. Nobody disputes any of this. A public official must decide whether to tell the town, and in what words. Evacuation costs money and credibility; silence has, elsewhere, ended in prosecutions.

The barrier and the century

A tidal barrier protects a capital city and was designed for a sea level rise that current projections have already overtaken at the upper bound. Its original design life ran to a date that is now inside the planning horizon, and the agency that operates it judges that, as built, it can hold some four decades beyond that. Two paths are open: strengthen what stands so it takes higher water, or build a replacement further downstream. The plan governing the estuary is deliberately adaptive and defers the choice between them by about two decades, on the reasoning that the projections will have narrowed by then. No single budget cycle can fund either path. Projections for 2100 differ by a factor of three depending on ice-sheet assumptions that no observation can yet settle.

The test that read wrong

On a deepwater well, a negative pressure test is run to confirm that the cement barrier holds. The drill pipe reads about fourteen hundred pounds per square inch while the two lines running to the blowout preventer read zero. The two readings cannot both describe a sealed well. One crew reads the discrepancy as a failed barrier. Another offers an explanation under which the lines behave differently from the pipe, and on that reading the test is a pass. Pressed to state the mechanism, nobody on the rig can name one, and it is not in any manual on board. Displacement is scheduled to begin in two hours, and the rig is forty-three days behind schedule at high daily cost.

Week three

A respiratory pathogen with no prior human circulation has been reported in one country. Cases are being counted, but the denominator is not: testing capacity reaches only those sick enough to present at hospital, so the case fatality rate is estimated from hospitalised patients alone and the number actually infected cannot be derived from what is being counted. The figure will move, and the direction it moves depends on which unknown resolves first. Whether the pathogen transmits before symptoms appear is unknown and will remain unknown for at least a month, and that single fact decides whether screening at borders can work at all. Border and school measures must be decided this week or not at all: imposed later they arrive after the thing they were meant to stop, and imposed now they will be judged against a count that still looks small.

Round 0: what makes this hard?

Read your Case card. The seven regimes of uncertainty are listed below, and the printed mat on your table names the same seven. In silence and on your own, name which one or two your case most likely sits in, and what makes you say so. 140 characters. Then, as a group, send the one you keep.

Aleatory variability

what is this?

Aleatory means the spread is in the process, not in your knowledge of it. Think of two courts handing down different sentences in cases that look the same: no fact was hidden from either bench, and reading one file more carefully would not have closed the gap. Scatter like this is a property of the system, so the answers that work are the ones built to absorb it, such as spare capacity, redundancy and insurance, and not one more study. Frame a case here when everyone already agrees on the range and is arguing about who carries it.

Calculable risk

what is this?

You can put a number on it and say where the number came from, because there is a pile of comparable past cases and the world that produced them is still the world you are standing in. That last part is what separates a real probability from a guess in a suit. Where it holds, weighing expected costs against expected benefits is a fair way to choose. Frame a case here when someone can say out loud what the comparable cases are and why the record still applies.

Parametric uncertainty

what is this?

Everyone agrees on how the pieces fit together and argues about the numbers you put in: a rate, a percentage, a cut-off. Because the frame is shared, the argument can be settled the boring way, by trying each number across the range people defend and watching whether the answer actually changes. Often it does not, and then the argument was never the decision. Frame a case here when the experts would draw the same diagram and fill in different figures.

Model uncertainty

what is this?

Two explanations fit the same history and point to different futures. The argument is about how the thing works, so digging up more of the past does not settle it: both stories already explain that past. Averaging them hides the disagreement behind one confident-looking figure. What helps is asking which action you would not regret under either story. Frame a case here when the experts describe two different mechanisms and each can show the record fits.

Deep uncertainty

what is this?

Three things can be missing when a decision has to be made: an agreed account of how the system works, agreed odds for what might happen, and agreed terms for what counts as a good outcome. Any one of them missing is already hard. This is the layer where all three are missing at once, so there is nothing left to optimise, and the question stops being "which option scores best?" and becomes "which option can we live with, whatever turns out to be true?". Frame a case here when even what counts as a good outcome is contested.

Radical uncertainty, catastrophic loss

what is this?

Almost all of the damage sits in the rare, huge event, so the average is the wrong thing to look at. Height behaves like the ordinary case: one more person in the room barely moves the average. Wealth does not, because one billionaire moves it entirely. Where losses behave like wealth, the record looks calm right up until it does not, since the events that matter have not happened yet. What you can still control is how exposed you are. Frame a case here when the worst plausible outcome, and not the typical one, is what the decision must answer to.

Catastrophic and irreversible

what is this?

Two things at once: the harm could not be undone, and waiting would teach you something. Either one alone is not this. When both hold, waiting is worth something in itself, on top of any benefit of the delay, for the same reason you do not knock down the wall while the survey is still coming back. Acting now spends a choice you cannot buy back later. It also says when to stop waiting, because once the information arrives the reason to hold is gone. Frame a case here when you can name what you would learn later and what could never be undone.
